#How to Become a Regular Writer - and an Expert in Your Very Own Niche

During a two-week summer holiday in France, I made a bet with myself: I would manage to become a regular writer on Medium, helping me dive into a niche of knowledge I've identified for myself.

Finding My Area Should Help Me Grow

While spending two weeks of summer vacation in the southern part of France, I recall the feeling when I left the office for the last time before the holiday: exhausted, tired, and dissatisfied. I generally enjoy my job as a senior project consultant in executive search. For several months, however, I've felt unfulfilled by what I do during my working hours. Once again in my life, I realized I wanted to identify a specific niche to develop into an expert and conquer "my" territory.

I came up with the idea of finding "my" area of expertise and writing about it in a way everyone understands. I don't want to write just one text or article; I aim to establish "my niche" as a topic to explore over a longer period. My wish is to become an expert in something, not necessarily like a university professor, but close.

Hoping to Dive Deep into One Specific Area of Knowledge

You might not know this feeling, but I've never experienced being a "real expert" in anything. My husband, for instance, is a plumber by education and now owns his company with employees and everything. He's an established expert in renovating heating systems, a crucial skill in Switzerland, given the still quite low outside temperatures in winter, despite the average temperature rise over recent years.

I thought about what I could do and realized I can write and work with foreign languages like English. So, I decided to write. As a first step, I read many articles on Medium about getting started, finding motivation and inspiration, and creating "my topic." It isn't easy.

Discovering What You Like and What You Know by Reflecting on My Past

I am a sociologist by education and have worked in HR for nearly the last 15 years. During that time, I've held several roles, mainly focusing on career development, career planning, CV checks, etc. I've never taken on a purely operational position, such as an HR Business Partner.

Looking back, I've always been fascinated by databases, data itself, systems, creating and updating systems, and thinking in structures. I'm interested in how data should be structured to analyze it and draw conclusions. This fascination might be connected to my minor at university in computational linguistics. Even before Siri, Alexa, ChatGPT & Co., we analyzed text datasets and applied statistical methods to predict phrases and sentences and find specific information without reading everything. I remember building a "pizza delivery service" in one lecture using a predecessor of today's chatbots. It asked what kind of pizza we would like and whether we wanted to add a drink to our order.

Linking the Past with Today's Life

Reflecting on this, I realized I wanted to combine this early interest and fascination with my experience in HR. Throughout my career, I've found that while HR might be a global setting, it is often very local and country-specific. Labor law, do's and don'ts, how to write a CV, whether a cover letter is needed, and how procedures work are more locally than globally organized.

What kind of questions and areas of expertise make sense when considering what I know from university, albeit slightly outdated, and the knowledge I've gained over the last 10 years of my professional career? Combined with today's technological situation, where do we land?

My intention now is to cover "HR - Gamification - Data - AI." I don't just want to write nice articles; I aim to come up with questions, find solutions, and figure out what's still possible. Perhaps I'll also explore what's not possible and why it isn't possible yet.
